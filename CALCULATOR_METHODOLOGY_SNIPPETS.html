How This Calculation Works

Formula:

Net Worth = Total Assets - Total Liabilities

✓ What This Includes:

  • • Liquid assets (cash, investments)
  • • Long-term assets (real estate, business)
  • • Retirement accounts (at current balance)
  • • All outstanding debts
  • • Debt-to-equity ratio visualization

⚠ What This Does NOT Include:

  • • Future capital gains tax liability
  • • Valuation adjustments for illiquid assets
  • • Tax basis differences
  • • Unfunded pension liabilities
  • • Contingent liabilities
Important: This calculation uses market values as entered. For a professional net worth analysis, have valuations professionally appraised and consider tax implications with a CPA.

How This Calculation Works

Formula (Future Value of Annuity):

FV = PV(1+r)ⁿ + PMT × [((1+r)ⁿ - 1) / r]
Where: PV = current savings, r = monthly rate, n = months, PMT = monthly contribution

✓ Assumptions Made:

  • • Consistent monthly contributions
  • • Monthly compounding
  • • Steady return rate (no volatility)
  • • No contributions missed
  • • No withdrawals until target date

⚠ What This Does NOT Include:

  • • Income taxes on interest earned
  • • Bank fees or account charges
  • • Life changes (job loss, emergencies)
  • • Inflation impact on purchasing power
  • • Market downturns or volatility
Reality Check: Actual savings often differs due to missed contributions, unexpected expenses, or market changes. Build in a safety margin—target 10-15% higher than calculated to account for real-world variability.

How This Calculation Works

Formula (Compound Interest with Regular Contributions):

FV = P(1+r)ⁿ + PMT × [((1+r)ⁿ - 1) / r]
Compounding: Monthly (12 times per year)
r = annual rate ÷ 12

✓ Key Features:

  • • Visualizes growth over time
  • • Shows contribution vs. growth breakdown
  • • Adjustable return assumptions
  • • Interactive time horizon (1-40 years)
  • • Demonstrates power of compound growth

⚠ What This Does NOT Show:

  • • Market volatility or downturns
  • • Investment fees or expenses
  • • Taxes on dividends/gains
  • • Inflation impact on purchasing power
  • • Actual year-by-year returns (smooth line)
Reality Check: This assumes smooth, consistent returns. In reality, markets fluctuate: years with +25% returns and years with -15% returns are normal. Time in market reduces timing risk. The growth shown is the long-term average, not the path you'll experience.

How This Calculation Works

Formula (Amortization):

M = P × [r(1+r)ⁿ] / [(1+r)ⁿ - 1]
M = Monthly payment, P = Principal, r = Monthly rate, n = Total payments

✓ What This Shows:

  • • Principal + Interest P&I payment
  • • Total interest cost over loan term
  • • Principal balance financed
  • • Down payment percentage
  • • Supports fixed-rate mortgages

⚠ CRITICAL - What This Does NOT Include:

  • ❌ Property taxes ($2K-$10K+/yr)
  • ❌ Homeowners insurance ($1K-$3K+/yr)
  • ❌ PMI if down payment <20% ($100-500/mo)
  • ❌ HOA fees ($200-500+/mo)
  • ❌ Maintenance reserves (1-2% of value)
⚠️ CRITICAL UNDERESTIMATION WARNING: This calculator shows only principal and interest. Your actual monthly cost will be 40-60% HIGHER when you include property taxes, insurance, and maintenance. For a $1M home, add $1,500-$2,500/month to the P&I shown here.
Next Steps: Use this P&I figure as a starting point only. Get actual quotes from lenders, property tax estimates from your local assessor, and insurance quotes to determine true monthly cost. Add 15-20% buffer for maintenance and unexpected repairs.

How This Calculation Works

Formula:

1. Future College Cost = Current Tuition × (1 + inflation)ⁿ
2. Accumulated Savings = FV(current savings) + FV(contributions)
3. Gap = Total Future Cost - Accumulated Savings

✓ What This Includes:

  • • Inflation adjustment for college costs
  • • Years until college starts
  • • Full study duration (typically 4 years)
  • • Investment returns during accumulation
  • • Surplus/shortfall calculation

⚠ What This Does NOT Include:

  • • Room and board/living expenses
  • • Books, supplies, equipment
  • • Financial aid or scholarships
  • • Student loan options
  • • Graduate school costs
Context: College inflation typically runs 2-3% annually above general inflation. Many students work, receive aid, or take loans. This calculator provides a baseline for savings planning—actual funding often combines savings, income, and strategic borrowing.
